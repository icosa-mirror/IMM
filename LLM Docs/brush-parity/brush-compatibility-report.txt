Open Brush to Quill and IMM brush compatibility
Consolidated research report — 2026-10-15

Purpose and current decisions

Authoritative format policy

1. Quill and Tilt files remain the authoritative authoring formats when native
   editability and reliable round-tripping are required.
2. IMM conversion and reverse conversion are best effort. Preserve useful native
   stroke editing and appearance where feasible, but exact recovery of original
   authoring inputs is not a requirement for this converter.
3. Do not add authoring-provenance payloads or complicate IMM merely to guarantee
   lossless round-tripping. The provenance discussion below describes an option
   that would be needed for such a guarantee, not planned or required work.
4. Existing fidelity, no-mesh-fallback and playback-performance constraints remain.

1. Identify Open Brush brushes that can become editable Quill/IMM paint strokes.
   Preserve paths, widths, colours, opacity and stroke identity. Do not use model
   layers, arbitrary mesh fallback, or meshes disguised as many tiny paint strokes.
2. Be pragmatic: A is a strong visual match candidate; B allows small disclosed
   differences; C is outside the current shortlist. These are source assessments,
   not visual certification. Fidelity tier and implementation route are separate.
3. Lit shaders are out of scope. Texture sampling is also outside the current
   implementation scope. A textured brush qualifies only if its important
   appearance can be reproduced by native stroke data or a small analytic feature.
4. Playback performance is an acceptance gate. Ordinary files must retain their
   existing path and performance. No native extension has been implemented or
   performance-approved. Texture cost has been assessed, not measured; its
   potential expense must not be presented as an established reason to reject it.
5. No converter, Unity compile, visual round-trip or playback benchmark was run.
   A proposed safe drawing mode is not currently implemented.

Existing-format shortlist — ten candidates

1. A — QuillRibbon -> Ribbon: transfer native stroke data with frame checks.
2. A — QuillCylinder -> Cylinder: matching seven-sided cross-section.
3. A — QuillEllipse -> Ellipse: seven sides and 0.3 minor/major radius ratio.
4. A — QuillCube -> Cube: matching square dimensions; Open Brush's sqrt(2) radius
   multiplier and -pi/4 phase account for the square's corner distance.
5. A — DoubleTaperedMarker -> Ribbon: bake CPU smoothing and the shader's static
   sin(pi*u) vertex-offset displacement into final ribbon edges. Do not assume
   final width is raw pressure width multiplied by the sine envelope.
6. B — Wire -> Cylinder: seven instead of eight sides and modest cap differences
   are accepted. Do not extend IMM solely to reproduce the eighth side. Preserve
   centreline/radius/colour and use compatible end samples where useful.
7. B — Marker -> Ribbon: correct visible width and optionally centre offset.
8. B — Marker Geometry -> Ribbon: the same mask-envelope approach as Marker.
9. B — TaperedMarker -> Ribbon: bake the visible taper into width/centre samples.
10. B — TaperedHueShift -> Ribbon: bake taper and static along-stroke hue into
    native widths/centres and per-point RGB. No new IMM feature is required.

1. Keep breaks, units, colour-space conversion and opacity correct. Disable
   rotational opacity for ordinary always-visible Open Brush paint.
2. Match broad silhouettes and characteristic effects rather than demanding
   pixel identity. Keep sample counts modest; do not recreate texel-scale detail
   with dense geometry. Additional samples still have playback cost.
3. Baked output remains native editable paint, but the source shader's behavior
   under subsequent length or pressure edits is not automatically preserved.
4. Coverage/dither differs between renderers. Opacity-data compatibility is not
   a guarantee of identical screen pixels, particularly under different MSAA.

Texture and shader findings

1. Marker and Marker Geometry: RGB is white; authored alpha cutoff is 0.067.
   Texel-column inspection gives visible width 0.9375–0.94921875 of the strip,
   with a small centre offset. The earlier approximately 0.57 measurement used
   cutoff 0.5 and is not relevant to these materials. Width estimates do not
   reproduce filtered runtime sampling exactly.
2. TaperedMarker: white-RGB mask, cutoff 0.067, one visible band per inspected
   column. Bake the principal taper, permitting fine contour/asymmetry differences.
3. TaperedHueShift: white-RGB 512x256 mask, cutoff 0.5, one smooth tapered band.
   Rounded broad end narrows towards the other end. Shader is unlit and static.
   Its vector-to-scalar expressions select the first component: effective colour
   weight is saturate(4*(u-0.5)^2), along U, not across V. Shifted RGB is
   hue06_to_base_rgb(r*(5+r)), with r the shader-space interpolated input red.
   Bake in the correct colour space; allow modest interpolation differences.
4. Taffy: cutoff 0.5 and a four-row atlas, with disconnected bands and substantial
   contour changes. A single width envelope would erase characteristic structure.
   It is not admitted merely because its shader is unlit.
5. Light: one 512x256 nearly white RGB texture with an irregular paint-like alpha
   mask, holes and rough edges. m_TextureAtlasV is 1: no row-atlas selection.
   Bloom.shader samples once and multiplies emitted RGB by the sampled alpha.
   The texture mapping is simple; the mask detail is defining. Light is excluded.
   A smooth luminous ribbon is not an accepted B-tier substitute.
6. SoftHighlighter: white-RGB 512x256 mask describing a smooth soft tapered mark,
   broad/rounded at one end and narrow at the other. Unlike Light, there are no
   defining paint-like holes or colour detail. It is a conditional B-tier candidate
   with additive blending plus analytic across-width softness; see below.
7. A crude maximum-row/column separability probe produced alpha MAE 0.1049 for
   SoftHighlighter and 0.4204 for Light. That constant-width probe was not a fit
   of a variable-width tapered ribbon and must not be used to reject the proposed
   SoftHighlighter approximation. Highlighter's inspected alpha was separable.
8. DotMarker: white-RGB circular mask at cutoff 0.5. A later editable stamp adapter
   may approximate each dot with a few width samples, preserving source placement
   and grouping. It is not admitted as a continuous ribbon, and no adapter exists.

Square and excluded lit brushes

1. Experimental SquarePaper uses SquareBrush and a lit StandardSingleSided graph.
   Its geometry is rectangular, height/width = 0.375. Every vertex receives UV
   (0.5,0.5), so assigned textures are sampled at a constant location; texture
   detail is not the exclusion reason. Lighting is. A rectangle parameter could
   represent the geometry with four vertices per section, but is not proposed
   solely for this excluded brush.
2. Experimental 3D Printing Brush is separate: Square3DPrintBrush produces beveled
   square sections/caps and uses FlatLit.shader. It is also excluded as lit.
3. QuillCube is the existing unlit square-section candidate already shortlisted.
4. Flat, Wire (Lit), FacetedTube, DoubleTaperedFlat, Lofted, Spikes, Icing,
   LightWire and lit/normal-mapped paint remain out of this investigation.
5. Hull/membrane brushes, including UnlitHull, have no admitted native editable
   stroke mapping. The prohibited model/mesh route must not be used as fallback.

Small optional native features and near misses

1. Additive blending — first candidate: TubeAdditive. Its shader uses One/One,
   ZWrite Off, Cull Off, default-white texture with no material texture assigned.
   With audio disabled, reuse B-tier Cylinder geometry and additive colour/opacity.
   Highlighter also nearly qualifies: SrcAlpha/One and a white-RGB mask whose
   hard silhouette can be baked into width. Its cutoff 0.12 applies to
   vertexAlpha*textureAlpha before final material opacity; preserve that distinction.
2. Soft opacity profile — SoftHighlighter. Bake broad taper into widths and
   lengthwise end fades into point opacity; evaluate a simple across-width soft
   band in an optional shader and blend SrcAlpha/One. Disable audio-reactive
   colour/geometry. No texture sample is needed for the proposed B-tier match.
   Visual and performance acceptance remain pending.
3. Multiplicative blending — Dark. Uses DstColor/Zero, ZWrite Off, no brush texture
   sampling. LineWithFadeBrush's lengthwise fades can use native point opacity;
   transverse edge fade can share the analytic softness feature. Shader factor is
   lerp(white, brushColour, k), where k saturates vertexAlpha*opacity*alphaMultiply*
   softMultiply*edgeFade. This is not ordinary alpha blending.
4. Emissive gain — an optional scalar could retain brightness beyond stored RGB.
   This does not unlock Light by itself. Validate colour space, render-target range
   and host tone mapping. Bloom is separate; no fullscreen bloom pass is proposed.
5. Explicit authored frames — optional data decoded into existing mAxU/mAxV at
   drawing preparation could preserve tilted sections and important bend geometry.
   Matching topology could keep existing GPU strides, shaders and draw counts.
   It is an opportunity, not a demonstrated performance-neutral implementation.
6. Additive variants must handle alpha once. Do not multiply RGB by alpha and
   also apply ordinary paint alpha-to-coverage. Keep depth testing, choose correct
   depth writes, and preserve mixed blend/layer order. Additive strokes commute
   within their group; additive and multiplicative groups generally do not.
7. Avoid per-stroke materials/draws and per-frame reconstruction. Investigate
   TubeAdditive, then Highlighter, then SoftHighlighter, then Dark. Leave Light,
   animated procedural shaders and all lit shaders excluded.
8. Runtime alpha-mask sampling/atlases remain outside the current implementation
   scope. General textures, PBR, arbitrary profiles and brush programs are not
   proposed. The separate cost assessment below does not authorize such changes.

Normal drawing frames and format compatibility

1. Standard ribbons use ComputeSurfaceFrameNew, whose cross products make width
   perpendicular to movement. Standard tubes use ComputeMinimalRotationFrame,
   aligning forward with the tangent. Arbitrary controller tilt is not generally
   retained in their section planes.
2. QuillFlatBrush and QuillTubeBrush override those defaults, taking controller
   right/up or orientation directly. Tilt is possible in normal use, without a
   plugin. Disabling plugins alone does not align these particular brushes.
3. IMM derives the tangent by averaging normalized neighboring directions, then
   U = normalize(cross(normal,tangent)), V = normalize(cross(tangent,U)). Different
   smoothing/tangents can change bends. Accept small differences; investigate
   visible distortion. A safe drawing mode could align the frame conventions.
4. Explicit-frame metadata needs versioning and an editor/import representation;
   do not repurpose mDir, the drawing-time view direction. Current paint importer
   rejects metadata versions greater than 1. Encoding is undecided.
5. Extended native IMM is not automatically readable as ordinary Quill. Keep
   standard Quill and extended IMM targets explicit, without silently exporting
   extended semantics as Quill-safe. Geometry, opacity and shader behavior all
   need verification, not just the brush GUID.

Texture-support performance assessment — no implementation authorized

1. No slowdown percentage or milliseconds has been measured. One optional unlit
   mask sample could be inexpensive; assuming it is prohibitively expensive is
   unsupported. Texture detail and sampling cost are different questions.
2. A narrow feature would add one shared mask sample and UV interpolation only
   on textured strokes. Existing applicable paint already samples blue noise for
   coverage. An extra lookup does not imply twice the frame time.
3. Uncompressed 512x256 R8 is 128 KiB, approximately 171 KiB with full mipmaps;
   RGBA8 is 512 KiB, approximately 683 KiB with mips, per unique image, not stroke.
   Compression and allocation alignment can change actual memory use.
4. Illustrative UV storage: adding 4 bytes changes an Android static point from
   28 to 32 bytes (+14.3% record size), or a pretessellated vertex from 20 to 24
   (+20%). These are not frame-time estimates or a required design. Section indices
   can generate across-width coordinates; along-stroke data needs separate design.
5. Sampling cost depends on shaded coverage, overlap, filtering and cache reuse.
   A shared small mask could have good locality. Image byte size alone does not
   predict bandwidth. Additive blending, disabled depth writes and bloom are
   separate costs; do not charge all of them to texture sampling.
6. Preserve untextured shaders/strides. Optional variants and shared images could
   avoid per-stroke binds/draws, though layer/material grouping may split batches.
   Existing fragment coverage means generic alpha-cutout penalties cannot simply
   be counted as entirely new costs.
7. Analytic avoidance is not automatically faster than a cached sample, particularly
   if it uses expensive math or adds many geometry samples. A future diagnostic
   should compare constant and sampled masks on identical geometry/blending, both
   sparse and overlapping, before matched complete-scene runs.
8. Primary guidance: https://developer.android.com/games/optimize/materials and
   https://developer.android.com/android-performance-analyzer/analyze/texture-mem-bw.
   These support bandwidth/overdraw attribution, not a numerical IMM prediction.

Playback acceptance and validation

1. Existing files must not become slower. Extended/converted workloads must also
   meet the playback requirement; richer appearance does not grant a slowdown
   allowance. Inconclusive timing is not proof of neutrality.
2. Before prototyping, identify a visible interaction, representative device/backend
   and input. Record baseline binary/input hashes, resolution, stereo, MSAA,
   sample/triangle counts and batches. Compare one independent change at a time.
3. Measure CPU and GPU frame time separately, tails/missed budgets, draw calls,
   upload stalls and memory. Include loading/seeking during playback and bounds/
   culling. Report loading and editing rebuild costs separately. Do not interfere
   with another agent's shared benchmark turn or run heavy builds during it.
4. Start with one diagnostic run; only expand promising experiments. Web work must
   follow code/projects/web/performance/INVESTIGATION_PROCEDURE.md, including
   matched Gallery interactions, warmup and alternating complete playback passes.
5. Preserve native editability, timing, content and A/B-appropriate appearance.
   Keep prototypes out of production until accepted. Stop or revise regressions;
   do not inflate stroke samples merely to chase texture detail or pixel identity.
6. No performance experiment was run in this session; all cost statements above
   are source-grounded hypotheses or arithmetic, not measured acceptance.

Reverse conversion from IMM to Tilt stroke data

1. Distinguish editable visual reconstruction from original authoring-data recovery.
   Native IMM can support the former for the ten candidates. Render-oriented paint
   data alone cannot generally recover the original Tilt control-point records.
2. Tilt StrokeData holds brush GUID, base brush size/scale, base colour, flags,
   grouping and control points. Control points have position, quaternion orientation,
   pressure and creation timestamp. Per-point colour overrides may also be present.
   StrokeData also has a generation seed, explicitly documented as not currently
   serialized; do not claim seed identity is guaranteed by existing Tilt files.
3. IMM paint stores positions, an orientation normal, drawing-view direction,
   per-point colour/opacity and width plus cross-section type. It does not directly
   retain the Open Brush GUID, original base-size/pressure decomposition, complete
   original controller quaternions, dropped source samples or their timestamps.
   Derived stroke timing is not the original controller sample timing. Normal,
   width and colour encoding/quantization further preclude exact numeric recovery.
4. Open Brush PressuredSize uses baseSize * lerp(minPressureScale,1,pressure), with
   unit/scale factors. Width alone does not identify both original size and pressure.
   A reverse converter can choose a consistent base size and synthesize pressure.
   It can also build a canonical quaternion from path tangent and normal, but that
   is a replacement orientation, not recovery of the original controller pose.
5. Existing Quill import demonstrates reconstruction: Quill.cs chooses base size
   from maximum width, derives pressure from width, constructs orientation from
   path tangent/normal, and generates timestamps using time++. It imports editable
   strokes, not original Tilt authoring records.
6. QuillRibbon/Cylinder/Ellipse/Cube: strongest visual reconstruction candidates.
   Target section data directly supports corresponding Open Brush Quill brushes,
   subject to prior frame/coverage limitations. Original raw pose/pressure/timing
   is still not preserved automatically.
7. Wire: good approximate visual reconstruction if identified as Wire. Returning
   to the original eight-sided brush can restore the customary appearance from
   path/radius, but frame/smoothing/cap details remain approximate. Plain IMM Circle
   does not identify whether its source was Wire or QuillCylinder.
8. Marker and Marker Geometry: good approximate editable ribbon reconstruction.
   Returning to the original brush requires reversing visible-width/centre baking
   and handling its texture/CPU smoothing. Plain IMM Ribbon does not preserve which
   source brush was used. Using a QuillRibbon preserves the baked appearance more
   directly, with different source-brush editing behavior.
9. DoubleTaperedMarker, TaperedMarker and TaperedHueShift: baked native strokes can
   be reconstructed visually as QuillRibbon strokes with widths and point colours.
   Recovering original-brush control points is substantially less reliable: taper,
   mask envelope, smoothing and colour transformations were applied during export.
   Reapplying the original shader to baked data would apply effects twice. At
   zero-width taper ends, original pressure/width information may be unrecoverable.
   TaperedHueShift's base colour may be estimated in suitable cases (the centre U
   has zero hue-mix weight), but this is not a general lossless inverse.
10. Exact preservation for an unmodified round trip would require optional
    authoring provenance: original source stroke records, brush identifiers and
    enough layer/transform context, associated with the exported stroke. Store
    original samples rather than hoping to invert smoothing/baking. This is a
    proposed editor/export concern, not an implemented IMM format feature.
11. Such provenance need not add shader work or GPU attributes. A versioned optional
    block could be skipped by playback, but file size, loading/decompression, older
    reader behavior and mapping to IMM strokes would still need design/validation.
    Do not claim literally zero loading cost or automatic Quill compatibility.
12. After an IMM stroke is edited, saved original records become historical data.
    Unchanged strokes can be restored from provenance; edited strokes need reverse
    conversion or a defined source-aware edit mapping. Exact original-data recovery
    cannot be promised for arbitrary edits merely by retaining a metadata block.
13. Assessment is code-based, not a tested round trip. Primary sources are Open
    Brush Assets/Scripts/StrokeData.cs, PointerManager.cs, Brushes/BaseBrushScript.cs
    and Quill.cs, plus IMM's exporter paint Point fields and serialization.
